**Target Audience:** SOC Leads, Forensic Analysts, Compliance Officers, Legal Counsel  
**Regulatory Context:** Digital Personal Data Protection Act (DPDPA), Indian Evidence Act Section 65B / Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA) 2023 Section 63

## 1. Candidate Infrastructure vs. Conclusive Human Attribution

A foundational tenet of MailSuraksha AI is **forensic honesty**:

> **Forensic Notice:**  
> Origin IP addresses, Autonomous System Numbers (ASNs), and geolocation coordinates extracted from email headers represent **candidate network infrastructure and probabilistic routing leads**, NOT conclusive proof of human physical presence, authorship, or legal identity.

### Why Conclusive Human Attribution Requires Judicial Process:
1. **Network Address Translation (NAT) & CGNAT:**  
   Multiple residential and cellular subscribers share a single public IPv4 address simultaneously. Establishing individual device identity requires carrier-grade NAT allocation logs from the serving ISP corresponding to the microsecond timestamp.
2. **Anonymization Infrastructure (VPNs, Tor, Proxies):**  
   Adversaries frequently route outbound traffic through commercial VPN providers, bulletproof hosting proxies, or Tor exit nodes. The candidate origin IP reflects the egress interface, not the threat actor's origin terminal.
3. **Untrusted Received Headers & Non-Proof of Forgery:**  
   `Received` headers are untrusted input. While the engine identifies **anomalies and consistency failures** (such as non-monotonic timestamps, private-to-public routing inversion, and syntax malformations), it does not claim to conclusively prove header forgery from header syntax alone without corroborating server-side transaction logs.
4. **Legal Admissibility Prerequisite:**  
   Conclusive legal attribution requires court-issued subpoenas or Section 91 CrPC notices to telecommunication service providers to correlate public IP/port timestamps with verified subscriber identities.

## 2. Technical Limitations Across Forensic Layers

### 2.1 Forwarding & Mailing List DKIM/SPF Breakage
- **Indirect Mail Flows:** When an email is forwarded through an intermediary (e.g., automated university forwarding, mailing list managers such as Mailman or ezmlm):
  - **SPF:** Fails unless the forwarder implements Sender Rewriting Scheme (SRS) to update the RFC 5321 envelope `MAIL FROM`.
  - **DKIM:** If the intermediary modifies the email body (e.g., appending an unsubscribe footer or modifying subject lines), the cryptographic body hash (`bh=`) check fails even though the original message was benign.
- **MailSuraksha Mitigation:** The pipeline evaluates DMARC alignment status in conjunction with `Authentication-Results` generated by internal edge MX servers to distinguish benign forwarding breaks from deliberate spoofing attempts.

### 2.2 BGP Dynamic Routing & Geolocation Resolution Accuracy
- IP geolocation databases (e.g., MaxMind, IPinfo) operate at city and regional granularities with varying accuracy:
  - Country-level accuracy: ~99%
  - City-level accuracy: ~75% - 85%
  - Cellular IP accuracy: Often resolves to the mobile operator's gateway MSC rather than the device's physical cell tower.
- Dynamic BGP route announcements and IP block reallocations can introduce temporary regional inaccuracies.

### 2.3 Threat Intelligence Reputation Lag & Zero-Day Domains
- Newly registered domains (NRDs) and dedicated fast-flux IP pools utilized in zero-day spear-phishing campaigns may initially have zero negative reports in public threat intelligence databases (AbuseIPDB, VirusTotal).
- **MailSuraksha Calibration:** When threat intelligence feeds return clean or unavailable scores, the ML risk engine elevates weights on domain lexical entropy, domain registration age, and RFC alignment violations to catch zero-day attacks without artificially penalizing benign domains when threat feeds are unavailable.

## 3. Recommended Standard Operating Procedures (SOP) for Analysts

1. **Maintain Chain of Custody:** Always store raw `.eml` evidence files alongside their pre-parse SHA-256 cryptographic verification hashes.
2. **Never Execute Automated Network Sanctions Solely on Candidate Origin IP:** Blocking single public IPs without checking whether the IP belongs to a shared cloud service provider (e.g., AWS, Azure, Google Cloud) can lead to unintended denial of service for legitimate traffic.
3. **Escalate to Judicial Inquiry for High-Value Incidents:** In high-impact BEC or corporate extortion cases, present MailSuraksha's Section 65B Evidence Documentation Package to law enforcement cyber cells to initiate ISP subscriber log preservation.
